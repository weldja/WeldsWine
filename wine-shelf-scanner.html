How the wine shelf scanner works

  1. Photograph the shelf

    Take one wide photo of the shelf, then a closer photo of each row so the price tags are sharp. Up to six photos, and they can overlap.

  2. Every wine and price is read

    All your photos are read together. Each wine is matched to its price tag, including loyalty card and offer prices, and listed once even if it appears in several photos.

  3. See which wines are worth it

    You get the three best-value wines with a plain reason for each, the wines that aren’t worth their price, and your own photo coloured from good value to poor.

What you get from one scan

The three best-value wines
Ranked best first, each with a short, plain reason, such as a well-made wine on offer well below its usual price.
The wines you’re paying for the name
Up to three wines that cost more than they’re worth, usually because of the brand on the label.
A value heat map of your own photo
Each wine on your shelf photo glows from green to red, so you can see the good buys at a glance and walk straight to them.
Clubcard and Nectar prices
Tell it you have a loyalty card and it judges value on the price you’ll actually pay.
Red, white or rosé
Only after a white tonight? Choose a colour and the picks come only from that colour.
Every wine on the shelf
A full list of what was found, cheapest first, with a value score out of 10 and a note on anything worth checking.

Honest about what it can and can’t do

The Shelf Scanner uses AI (Claude, made by Anthropic) to read your photos and judge value. It reads small price tags well, but glare, blur or a tag hidden behind a bottle can still trip it up.

So every wine is marked with how sure the reading is, anything doubtful is flagged for you to check, and it never makes up a price it couldn’t read. If it doesn’t know a wine, it says so. Always check the shelf tag before you buy.

Questions

Which shops does the Shelf Scanner work in?

It isn’t tied to any one shop. It works from your photos, so if the bottles and the price tags are in the picture, it can read them. That includes supermarkets such as Tesco, Sainsbury’s, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Co-op, Aldi and Lidl, as well as independent wine shops.

Does it use Clubcard and Nectar prices?

Yes. Many shelves show a normal price and a loyalty card price. Tick the loyalty card box before you scan and the Shelf Scanner judges value using the loyalty price. Leave it unticked and it uses the normal price.

How does it decide what is good value?

It compares what each wine usually is, and what it usually costs in the UK, with the price on this shelf. Each wine gets a value score from 1 to 10. A good wine on offer below its usual price scores well. A wine that costs more mainly because of its brand scores poorly. If it doesn’t know a wine, it says so rather than guessing.

How accurate is it?

It reads small price tags well, but it can still misread a price, especially with glare, blur or a tag that is partly hidden. Every wine is marked with how confident the reading is, and anything doubtful is flagged for you to check. Always check the shelf tag before you buy.

How many photos should I take?

Between one and six photos of the same shelf. One wide shot plus a close-up of each row works best. Overlapping photos are fine: they help double-check the prices, and a wine seen in more than one photo is only listed once.

Can I look for just a red, a white or a rosé?

Yes. Choose Red, White or Rosé before you scan and the best-value picks only come from that colour. Every wine on the shelf is still listed, and you can filter the full list by colour.

Does it keep my photos?

The Shelf Scanner doesn’t save your photos. They are sent to the AI to be read, and the result comes back to your phone. The photos stay on your phone only until you start a new scan.
